Why k-medoids rather than an automatic “form detector”?

For two excerpts P and Q, their similarity is the mean of the similarities between audio-feature frames belonging to each excerpt: sim(P,Q) = mean S(i,j) for i in P and j in Q. We set d(P,Q) = 1 − sim(P,Q), and use a simple deterministic k-medoids procedure to assign excerpts to representative examples. This need not recover melodic phrasing or functional harmony, especially if the current descriptor is RMS or spectral centroid.

The novelty-delimited mode starts from peaks detected in section 3, enforces a minimum passage length, and then clusters those passages. Changing the detection threshold may therefore change the segmentation and the clustering. In equal-length mode the segmentation remains independent of novelty.

How does weighted late fusion work?

Each descriptor produces a similarity matrix with entries in the interval [0, 1]. Chroma uses cosine similarity of normalized pitch-class profiles. Scalar descriptors use exponential similarity based on robust within-recording scale estimates. For nonnegative weights normalized to sum to one, the combined matrix is S = wCSC + wESE + wBSB + wFSF. The same fused matrix drives the heatmap, novelty detection and passage-distance calculation for k-medoids. These normalizations make the controls interpretable but do not guarantee an improved musical grouping. Setting all but one weight to zero must reproduce that descriptor alone.

5 · Reflect: similarity is not form

Do similarly colored passages sound related? Does fusion improve on chroma alone, or introduce unwanted acoustic distinctions? What changes when you increase the number of groups? Do the clusters identify musical families or simply similar acoustic properties?

Core lesson. A cluster groups passages according to the chosen descriptor and comparison rule. Its label is an algorithmic category, not an authoritative designation of theme, phrase, or musical form.
Optional mathematical and technical explanation

The waveform is divided into non-overlapping 0.75-second comparison intervals. Within each interval, three short-window FFT spectra are extracted and summarized. RMS estimates signal energy; spectral centroid estimates the mean frequency weighted by spectral magnitude; chroma groups spectral energy into twelve pitch classes; positive spectral flux estimates sudden changes in magnitude spectra. Chroma here is an approximate, educational audio estimator; strong harmonics and piano resonance can contribute to neighboring or harmonic pitch classes.

For feature vectors fi, each matrix element is a similarity S(i,j). Chroma uses cosine similarity, while scalar features use exponentially decreasing similarity with a scale determined by the feature’s distribution. For novelty at time t, a checkerboard contrasts within-side similarities with cross-boundary similarities in a context of ±k frames. Values are normalized for display; local maxima exceeding the selected relative threshold are highlighted. This is a basic Foote-style novelty detector. Module 5 additionally groups audio passages through a deterministic k-medoids algorithm applied to their pairwise similarities.

Sampling at fixed seconds (rather than musical beats) means expressive tempo changes can distort diagonal repetitions. Audio–beat alignment and tempo-invariant representations will be discussed in later modules.
